## Week 1 (Apr 2) — POSTED

**"39% wish they spent more"**

Angle: Counterintuitive stat from Real Estate Witch 2022 survey (1,000 new construction buyers). 39% wish they'd spent MORE on upgrades, not less. Tied to Dennis Webb / Fulton Homes quote from Builder Marketing Podcast (hosted by Greg Bray, 1st connection). Tagged @Greg Bray.

```
39% of new home buyers wish they'd spent more on upgrades.

More. Not less.

A 2022 consumer survey of 1,000 new construction buyers found that more people regret playing it safe than going big. Makes sense when most design studio visits are 2-4 hours and you're staring at a 2-inch swatch trying to imagine a whole kitchen. Of course you pick the safe option.

Dennis Webb from Fulton Homes said it well on @Greg Bray's Builder Marketing Podcast: "Most builders are product-centric. A retail builder takes the customer and puts the customer at the center of the universe."

That 39% is what happens when the process isn't built around the buyer.
```

Source: realestatewitch.com/buying-new-construction-home-2022-data/
Podcast: Builder Marketing Podcast Ep 312, "Embracing a Retail Mindset in Home Building" (Greg Bray + Kevin Weitzel + Dennis Webb, Fulton Homes VP)

## Future Post Ideas (Backlog)

### "I Looked at 50 Builder Websites"
Builder websites are bad. Don't name anyone. "45 of 50 had no way to preview a single upgrade online. The most important revenue moment lives on a printed PDF at the sales office." Pair with blurred screenshot collage.

### "The Design Center Manager"
Elevate design center managers. Nobody writes about them on LinkedIn. "The most important salesperson in homebuilding isn't the one who sells the home." Gets shared by DCMs, their VPs see it. Ends with "y'all should pay them more, too."

### "The Four-Hour PDF" (Origin Story)
Rashaad's personal design center story. 4 hours, printed PDF, spent 40% more than planned. Not pitched as founder journey. "I think about that appointment constantly." Establishes who he is.

### "Toll Brothers Spends Millions"
The accessibility gap. Toll has VR headsets. A 300-closing builder in Georgia has a PDF. The gap isn't talent, it's tools. SEC data ($203K upgrades/home, $2.2B revenue).
