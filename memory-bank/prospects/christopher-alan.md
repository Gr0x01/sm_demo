## Selections / Upgrade Process (CRITICAL)

### Previous assessment: "No selections" - THIS WAS WRONG

**Evidence that buyers DO have selections/options:**

1. **Pricing disclaimer on every floor plan page**: "Final price depends on homesite and **options selected**. Please contact for pricing." This appears on Sandpiper, St. Augustine, and Cape Coral model pages.

2. **NewHomeSource listing**: Floor plans described as "all complete with an array of upgrades to enrich your lifestyle."

3. **Multiple finish levels documented**:
   - "Luxe Collection" finishes: granite countertops, wood cabinets with soft-close doors, PGT Windows, stainless steel appliance package, tile throughout main living areas
   - Updated finishes: quartz countertops (upgrade from granite), Shaker-style cabinetry, upgraded plank tile flooring
   - 3-car garage upgrade available on select homesites

4. **Indeed job listing** referenced "one to two years of working experience in interior design for a single-family home builder or design industry" - they have/had design-related roles.

5. **Team page** lists "sales and design" as part of their service span.

6. **"Options for every budget"** - language on multiple pages suggests tiered offerings.

### What their selections process likely looks like:

Based on the evidence, CAH appears to operate a **limited-selection production model**:
- Pre-curated finish packages (likely 2-3 tiers: base, Luxe Collection, upgraded Luxe)
- Structural options (3-car garage, possibly lanai extensions)
- Color/finish selections within tiers (countertop colors, cabinet colors, tile selections)
- NOT a full design center with hundreds of individual SKUs - more of a "pick your package + some colors" model
- Some homes are built spec (move-in ready with pre-selected finishes), others are to-be-built where buyers get limited choices

### Why they were initially flagged "no selections":
The website does NOT prominently market selections or customization. There's no "design center" page, no "personalize your home" feature, no online configurator. The website sells the finished product, not the process. You'd only discover the options by visiting a sales office or catching the fine print disclaimers. This is common with production builders at the entry-level price point - they downplay customization to simplify the buying process.

## What Stands Out

1. **580 homes/yr with zero visualization tools** - that's significant volume at the entry-to-midrange price point ($264-530K). Even modest upgrade revenue per home ($5-10K estimated) = $2.9-5.8M in annual upgrade revenue. The ICP fit is strong.

2. **Rapid geographic expansion** - growing from SW Florida to East Central (Palm Bay) and West Central (Citrus Springs, Ocala, Spring Hill). Each new market needs to replicate the sales experience. Visualization scales better than flying design consultants to new markets.

3. **The Lennar-to-CAH pipeline** - Alana Consolo came from Lennar. Mike Bone came from D.R. Horton. These people know what national builders do with technology. They joined CAH for the culture/opportunity but are working with fewer tools.

4. **Limited selections model = perfect for Finch** - CAH doesn't need a 500-SKU Envision implementation. They need something that shows buyers the difference between their 2-3 finish packages in the actual room. That's exactly what Finch does at $500/mo vs $50K+/yr for enterprise tools.

5. **"Options for every budget" but no way to visualize the difference** - their website shows the upgraded finishes (quartz, shaker cabs) but a buyer looking at a $270K Sandpiper can't see what going from granite to quartz looks like in THEIR kitchen.
