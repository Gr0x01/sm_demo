On investigation, they run a mature Envision Options deployment with 3D room visualizers, per-floorplan kitchen/bath scenes, and real-time material swaps. Multiple cabinet styles (Savannah15 series), countertop, flooring, backsplash swaps in a 3D-rendered kitchen scene.

**Decision (2026-03-26):** Skip Fulton. They're well past the "nothing" stage. Finch's positioning is the step between nothing and Envision — for builders who can't justify a six-figure 3D visualization platform. Fulton is firmly in Envision territory.

## Design Center & Options Process

### Physical Design Center
- **Size**: 13,000 sq ft, South Tempe (1241 W. Warner Road)
- **Staff**: In-house designers (highly trained design consultants)
- **Process**: Every buyer gets a personal appointment with a design consultant. They first review your Design Online selections, then you interface with subject-matter experts in Cabinets, Appliances, and Structured Wiring.
- **Options count**: 2,900+ competitively priced items (not counting color variations). Some sources say 6,000+ choices.
- **Categories**: Cabinets, Appliances, Flooring, Countertops, Structural Wiring, Window Coverings, Doors, Lighting, Electrical fixtures, Ceiling fans, Bathroom fixtures, Kitchen fixtures, Outdoor kitchens, Garage systems, Closet systems, Fireplaces
- **Appliance brands**: Viking, Bertazzoni, GE
- **Flooring options**: Walnut, birch, bamboo, plank tiles (up to 48-inch)

### Browse Nights
- **Schedule**: Every 1st and 3rd Thursday evening of the month
- **Format**: No-pressure introduction to the Design Center. Designers available to answer questions and direct buyers to various options. Buyers can explore at their own pace before their formal appointment.

### Design Online (Envision Options)
- **URL**: edc2.envisionoptions.com/org-506/
- **Brand name**: "Fulton Homes Design Online" (trademarked with the TM symbol)
- **Powered by**: Envision Options (Zonda/BDX product)
- **Features**:
  - Design Checklist — browse products/options for your specific floorplan
  - Wishlist — save favorites
  - Room Visualizers — static or 360-degree scenes showing option swaps (countertops, cabinets, etc.)
  - Kitchen Design application — experiment with cabinetry, flooring, appliances
  - Bathroom design tool — customize layouts, colors, materials, finishes
  - Personal account — view home details, make selections, review pricing in advance
- **Pre-appointment workflow**: Buyers spend time on Design Online before their appointment. Designers access the buyer's portal and review selections prior to the visit, making it faster and smoother. (Similar to what Shea Homes does with Roomored — 3 hours online before appointment.)

## Key Differentiators They Market

1. **Retail DNA**: "We outsell other builders in our communities by 2-to-1." The entire org is built on retail merchandising principles from Ira Fulton's clothing empire.
2. **Zero Energy Ready**: DOE Zero Energy Ready certification at select communities. Big marketing differentiator.
3. **Philanthropy**: Ira Fulton donated $168M+ to ASU (engineering school named after him). $300M+ total to universities. BusinessWeek consistently ranked him among America's 50 most generous philanthropists. Earmarked 3/5 of company profit for philanthropy pre-recession.
4. **Transparency**: "Fulton Features" positioning — "many other builders simply give you the basics and leave you on the hook for pricy upgrades." They market included features aggressively to show value.
5. **Ultra-configurable plans**: Glennwilde community offers plans where you can add lofts, extra garage stalls, dual master bedrooms.

## Recession Note (Important Context)
During the housing downturn, Fulton Homes filed for bankruptcy but continued operations. They used the recession to: continue land development, maintain marketing, expand design centers into profit centers, retain top sales staff, and improve operational systems. The Design Center survived the recession because Webb made it a revenue generator, not just a cost center.

## The Gap (Finch Angle)

Fulton already has Envision, so they think they're covered on visualization. But:

1. **Envision shows generic 3D renders, not their actual rooms.** The Room Visualizer in Envision uses pre-rendered scenes that don't match their specific model homes. A buyer looking at the Prairie model's kitchen doesn't see THAT kitchen with their selections — they see a generic kitchen mockup.

2. **Their blog talks about buyers spending "countless hours on Houzz" to design their homes.** Buyers are already going to third-party sites to visualize. Finch could keep them in Fulton's ecosystem.

3. **The Design Online prep workflow is the entry point.** Buyers already spend time online before their appointment. If they could see their actual selections in the room during that prep time, they'd arrive at the appointment having already committed to higher-end options.

4. **Dennis Webb's retail instinct is the hook.** He merchandises homes like suits. The logical extension: you wouldn't sell a suit from a catalog with color swatches. You'd show the customer wearing it. Same with a kitchen.

5. **2,900+ options on a price sheet is overwhelming.** Even with Envision, most of those options are text + swatch. The upgrade from base granite to premium quartz is invisible on a screen until you see it in context.

## Flags

- **Already on Envision Options** — they may feel "solved." Deprioritize unless we have a specific angle.
- **Webb has been building this system for 28 years** — he's proud of it. Don't lead with "your tool is lacking."
- **Private/family-owned** — decisions happen fast but also personal. Don't disrespect the family legacy.
- **Bankruptcy history** — sensitive topic. Never reference.

## Outreach Strategy

**Target**: Dennis Webb (VP of Operations). He owns the Design Center, the selection experience, and the technology decisions around it. He's the buyer.

**Angle**: Don't compete with Envision head-on. Position Finch as the next layer: "Your Design Online gets buyers browsing. What if they could see those selections in the actual room before their Thursday browse night?"

**What NOT to say**: Don't call Envision basic. Don't say their system is lacking. Webb built it. He's proud of it.

**What to lean into**: His retail background. He ran clothing stores where customers tried things on. That's exactly what Finch does for kitchens.
