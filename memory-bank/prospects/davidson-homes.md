## Evermore Homes Acquisition (March 2025)

This is the big context for the outreach. Davidson acquired Evermore Homes to enter Arizona.

- **Closed**: March 10, 2025
- **What they got**: ~2,000 lots across 20 communities
- **States added**: Arizona (first time), plus additional presence in Alabama and Georgia
- **Evermore background**: Built through roll-up acquisitions of Grayhawk Homes (Georgia), Stoneridge Homes (Alabama), and Dorn Homes (Arizona)
- **Why sold**: CEO Lee Darnold cited capital constraints preventing execution of growth vision
- **Davidson's playbook** (per CEO Brandon Jones): "Establish an initial footprint through acquisition, integrate operations quickly, and then scale aggressively"
- **Evermore was a top-100 builder** before acquisition

Key implication for outreach: Davidson is integrating a newly acquired Arizona operation. They're standardizing processes, onboarding teams, and likely evaluating what tech to keep vs. replace. This is a classic "new division, fresh tooling decisions" window.

## Current Design Center / Visualization Process

### What We Know
- **Physical design center** in Prescott with samples (flooring, countertops, cabinetry, light fixtures)
- **In-person appointments** with interior designers
- **Davidson corporate claims "cutting-edge virtual design tools"** — but this appears to be generic marketing copy. No specific platform (Envision, Anewgo, ECI) was found on their website or in any press coverage.
- **No evidence of interactive visualization** on their website. Floor plan pages show exterior renderings and some interior model photos, but no online configurator or selection tool.
- **Evermore's design center page** returned a 404 — likely being transitioned to Davidson branding
- **Dorn Homes (predecessor)** had a traditional design center with "professional interior designers" and 11 model homes. Standard sample board approach.

### NoviHome (their "Davidson Homes App")
Davidson uses NoviHome as their buyer-facing mobile app (branded "Davidson Homes App, Powered by Novihome"). NoviHome is a buyer experience / CRM platform: construction progress updates, documents, milestones, community info, floor plan browsing, buyer communication portal, and activity analytics. It does NOT do visualization — no room rendering, no "see your selections in the kitchen." The selection experience is still sample boards and lists. Davidson is clearly tech-forward (investing in buyer-facing tools) but has a wide-open gap: they have the communication layer but not the visual selection experience.

### Assessment
The AZ design center is almost certainly a traditional sample-board operation inherited from Dorn/Evermore. Davidson's corporate design centers in the Southeast may have more sophisticated tooling, but the Arizona operation is newly acquired and likely running on Dorn's legacy process. They use NoviHome for buyer communication but nothing for visualization. This is the ideal moment to introduce Finch — they're tech-forward but haven't closed the visual gap.

Davidson's price points ($360K-$1.1M) span entry to move-up. Hidden Hills at $720K+ is competing with semi-custom, where upgrade expectations are highest.

## Outreach Angles

### 1. Post-Acquisition Integration Window
Davidson just absorbed Evermore/Dorn's Arizona operation. They're standardizing processes, evaluating tools, and setting up "the Davidson way" in a new market. This is the best time to introduce a design center tool — before they lock in on someone else's platform or default to the legacy Dorn process.

**Message frame**: "You're setting up Arizona right now. Here's a tool that makes your design center produce more revenue from day one."

### 2. Steve's Proptech Background
Snoddy sold HomeKey to builders. He knows the builder tech landscape. He's evaluated platforms. He understands ROI arguments. Don't pitch him like a tech skeptic — pitch him like a peer who gets it.

**Message frame**: "You've been on both sides of the builder tech conversation. This is the tool that actually moves the upgrade revenue number."

### 3. Revenue Per Home at High Price Points
Hidden Hills homes start at $720K. At that price, buyers expect a premium selection experience. A PDF option sheet or sample board feels incongruent with a $750K purchase. And the upgrade revenue opportunity is proportionally larger — $20K+ in upgrades on a $750K home is normal; getting it to $25K or $30K is the Finch value prop.

**Message frame**: "Your Hidden Hills buyers are spending $750K on a home. They shouldn't be picking their kitchen from a sample board."

### 4. Prescott Market Differentiation
Prescott is a competitive market with Mandalay, Taylor Morrison, and others. Davidson is the new entrant (via acquisition). An AI-powered visualization experience in the design center would differentiate them from established local competitors.

**Message frame**: "You're the new builder in Prescott. Make your design center the reason buyers choose you."

### 5. Design Center Manager Pain
The Prescott design center inherited from Dorn. Staff may be overwhelmed managing an acquisition transition while running appointments. A tool that makes appointments more efficient and productive helps the team that's under the most pressure right now.

## Risk Factors

1. **Snoddy may no longer be at Davidson.** His LinkedIn still shows Evermore. Post-acquisition leadership changes are common. He could have been let go or his role could have changed. Need to verify before reaching out.
2. **Arizona is new and small.** Davidson's focus is Southeast + Texas. Arizona might be a secondary priority where decisions get deferred. Counter: Snoddy was hired specifically for AZ, so it has attention.
3. **Corporate tooling decisions may be made at HQ.** Brooke Jock (VP Sales & Marketing) at corporate may dictate design center tools across all divisions. Counter: new divisions often have autonomy while being integrated.
4. **Price points in Prescott skew retirement.** Older buyers may have different upgrade behavior than young families. Counter: retirees often have more budget for upgrades and care deeply about finishes.
