## Data Points by Credibility Tier

### Tier 1: Independent / Third-Party Research

**3D Cloud / Provoke Insights Furniture Shopping Study (2026)**
- Publisher: Provoke Insights (independent market research firm), commissioned by 3D Cloud
- Sample: 400 U.S. consumers, Nov-Dec 2025
- Method: Random stratified sampling aligned with U.S. Census, +/-4.89% margin at 95% confidence
- Findings:
  - In-store shoppers using 3D tools with salespeople: **67% more likely to make $2,500+ purchases** (55% vs 33%)
  - **1 in 3 configurator users exceeded their original budget** vs 17% of non-users
  - 32% more likely to report "very satisfied" (74% vs 56%)
  - 62% said it remains challenging to visualize how furniture will look in their home
- Credibility: Medium-high. Independent firm conducted it, but 3D Cloud (a viz vendor) commissioned it. Furniture, not home building, but consumer psychology of visualization-to-purchase is directly transferable.

**NAR Profile of Home Staging (2021, 2023, 2025)**
- Publisher: National Association of Realtors
- Sample: ~1,200 real estate professionals (2025 edition)
- Findings:
  - 83% of buyers' agents: staging made it easier for buyers to visualize the property
  - 29% of agents: staging led to 1-10% increase in dollar value offered
  - 49% of agents: staging reduced time on market
- Credibility: High (independent). Analogy to visualization is strong (helping buyers see = increased spending) but not a direct comparison.

### Tier 2: Named Builder Case Studies (Credible but Vendor-Published)

**ECI Insearch / Signature Homes**
- Source: ECI case study, IBS brochure PDF
- Builder: Signature Homes (Tyler Belcher, EVP, named)
- Claims:
  - +20% sales increase
  - +10-15% profit increase
  - -75% change order reduction
  - Build time shortened (180 days to 120 days)
  - Design appointment time reduced by more than 2/3
- Credibility: Medium. Named builder + named exec, but published on vendor site. "Up to 15%" is a ceiling.

**Roomored / ILG / Shea Homes**
- Source: Builder Online article
- Builder: Shea Homes (Jeff Peterson, quoted by name)
- Claims:
  - Design appointment time reduced up to 50%
  - Fewer change orders, less rework/tear-outs
  - Options sales ~30% higher when online design tools are used (ILG aggregate data)
  - Buyers spend 3 hours on average online before design appointment
- Credibility: Medium. Named builder, VP-level quote. ILG's "30% higher" is aggregate from their network but methodology unknown.

**Higharc / Buffington Homes**
- Source: Higharc case study
- Builder: Buffington Homes
- Claims:
  - $10M additional revenue from increased home sales
  - $100K cost reduction from third-party fees
  - 75% reduction in time to market (3 months to 3 weeks)
  - 20 more homes closed than anticipated in 2023 (from 15-day schedule savings)
- Credibility: Medium-high. Specific dollars, specific outcomes. But this is about the full design-to-build platform, not visualization in isolation.

**Anewgo (Weitzel quote)**
- Source: OffsiteBuilder interview
- Claim: "Instantaneous increase in option sales of 40% in most cases"
- Credibility: Low-medium. VP Sales quote in a trade interview. No methodology.

**Aareas Interactive (VDC)**
- Source: aareas.com homepage
- Claims: 70% increase in upgrade sales, 67% sales & design efficiency gain, $250K+ savings per project
- Credibility: Low. Vendor website stats, no named builder, no methodology.

- Not statistically significant, but directionally interesting

## The Pattern Across All Data

Despite varying credibility, every single source points the same direction:

| What they measured | Range | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Upgrade/option revenue increase | 20-70% | ECI, Aareas, Anewgo, ILG |
| Design appointment time reduction | 30-67% | ECI, Roomored, Chameleon Power, CPS |
| Change order reduction | Up to 75% | ECI Signature Homes |
| Budget exceeded by visualization users | 1 in 3 (vs 1 in 6 without) | 3D Cloud study |
| High-ticket purchase likelihood increase | 67% more likely | 3D Cloud study |

No single data point is ironclad. But 10+ companies across 20+ years, using different technologies (3D renders, photo swaps, configurators), all reporting the same directional result? That's a pattern.

## What This Means for the Article
